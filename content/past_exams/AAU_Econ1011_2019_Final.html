1. Scarcity only affects poor countries, not rich countries.

False. Scarcity affects ALL societies, rich or poor. Even billionaires face scarcity (limited time). Scarcity means limited resources vs unlimited wants — this is universal.

2. The Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF) shifts outward when there is economic growth.

True. Economic growth (more resources, better technology) shifts the PPF outward, allowing the economy to produce more of both goods.

3. In a command economy, market forces of supply and demand determine prices.

False. In a COMMAND economy, the GOVERNMENT (central planners) determines prices. Market forces determine prices in a MARKET (capitalist) economy.

4. The Law of Demand states that price and quantity demanded have an inverse relationship.

True. When price increases, quantity demanded decreases (and vice versa). This inverse relationship creates the downward-sloping demand curve.

5. In the circular flow model, households produce goods and services.

False. HOUSEHOLDS provide FACTORS OF PRODUCTION (labor, land, capital). FIRMS produce goods and services.

6. GDP measures production within a country's borders, while GNP measures production by citizens.

True. GDP is geographic (within borders). GNP is nationality-based (citizens anywhere).

7. Inflation is a sustained DECREASE in the general price level.

False. Inflation is a sustained INCREASE in prices. DEFLATION is a sustained decrease.

8. Perfect competition achieves allocative efficiency (P = MC) in the long run.

True. Perfect competition is the only market structure that achieves allocative efficiency (P = MC) in long-run equilibrium.

9. The fundamental economic problem is:

Scarcity (limited resources vs unlimited wants) is THE fundamental economic problem. All other problems stem from it.

10. Examine the PPF diagram. Point A (on the curve) represents:

Point A lies ON the PPF curve, meaning all resources are fully and efficiently employed. B (inside) is inefficient, C (outside) is unattainable.

11. Opportunity cost is BEST defined as:

Opportunity cost is the SINGLE NEXT BEST alternative given up — NOT all alternatives (A), NOT money spent (C), NOT production cost (D).

12. In a CAPITALIST economy, basic economic questions are answered by:

In capitalism, the "invisible hand" of the market (supply and demand) determines what, how, and for whom to produce.

13. If consumer INCOME DECREASES (for a normal good), what happens to demand?

For NORMAL goods, lower income means less demand (curve shifts left). Inferior goods show the opposite (demand increases when income falls).

14. Given Qd = 100 - 2P and Qs = 20 + 2P, what is the equilibrium quantity?

Set Qd = Qs: 100 - 2P = 20 + 2P → 80 = 4P → P = 20. Then Q = 100 - 2(20) = 60 units.

15. When price is ABOVE equilibrium, the result is:

Above equilibrium: Qs > Qd (producers supply more, consumers demand less) = SURPLUS. Prices will fall to clear surplus.

16. If price increases by 10% and quantity demanded decreases by 20%, demand is:

PED = |%ΔQ / %ΔP| = |20% / 10%| = 2.0. Since PED > 1, demand is ELASTIC — consumers are very responsive to price changes.

17. A MONOPOLY is characterized by:

Monopoly = ONE seller + NO close substitutes + HIGH barriers to entry. B is perfect competition, C is oligopoly, D is monopolistic competition.

18. NDP (Net Domestic Product) equals:

NDP = GDP - Depreciation. Depreciation (capital consumption) is subtracted to get net production.

19. The unemployment rate formula is:

Unemployment rate = (Unemployed / Labor Force) × 100. Labor Force = Employed + Unemployed (actively seeking).

20. Examine the diagram. When AD shifts right, what type of inflation occurs?

When AD shifts RIGHT (increases), excess demand pulls prices UP = DEMAND-PULL inflation.

21. A firm sells 500 units at 20 Birr. Price falls to 15 Birr, quantity rises to 700. What is PED?

%ΔQ = (700-500)/500 = 40%. %ΔP = (15-20)/20 = -25%. PED = |40% / -25%| = 1.6 (Elastic).

22. In a market economy, "consumers are kings" refers to:

Consumer sovereignty means consumers "vote with money" — their preferences determine what gets produced.

23. Government decisions about TAXATION and SPENDING are called:

FISCAL policy = government taxation + spending. MONETARY policy = central bank (money supply, interest rates).

24. Monetary policy in Ethiopia is conducted by:

The National Bank of Ethiopia (NBE) controls monetary policy (interest rates, money supply). Ministry of Finance handles fiscal policy.

25. Technology replacing workers (e.g., ATMs replacing bank tellers) creates:

STRUCTURAL unemployment = skills mismatch (technology changes). Frictional = job search. Cyclical = recession. Seasonal = seasonal changes.

26. If MRS = 2, the consumer is willing to give up:

MRS = 2 means the consumer will give up 2 units of Y for 1 more unit of X (keeping same satisfaction).

27. In the LONG RUN, perfectly competitive firms earn:

Free entry/exit eliminates economic profit in the long run. Firms earn ZERO economic profit (but still normal profit).

28. Explain the difference between GDP and GNP. (3 points)

GDP measures production WITHIN a country's borders (regardless of who produces it). GNP measures production by a country's CITIZENS (regardless of where they produce). Example: A Chinese factory in Ethiopia counts in Ethiopia's GDP but China's GNP.

29. Name the TWO main macroeconomic policy instruments and who implements each. (3 points)

1. MONETARY POLICY — implemented by the CENTRAL BANK (NBE). Controls money supply and interest rates. 2. FISCAL POLICY — implemented by the GOVERNMENT (Ministry of Finance). Controls taxation and government spending.

30. Distinguish between MITIGATION and ADAPTATION as climate change responses. (3 points)

MITIGATION = Reducing greenhouse gas emissions (tackling the CAUSE). Examples: reforestation, renewable energy. ADAPTATION = Coping with climate change impacts (dealing with EFFECTS). Examples: drought-resistant crops, water harvesting.
