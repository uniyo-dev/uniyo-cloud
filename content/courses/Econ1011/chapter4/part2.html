4.2 Theory of Costs in the Short Run

4.2.1 Definition and Types of Costs

Cost is the monetary value of resources used in production. Understanding costs is essential for firms to make production decisions.

Types of Costs:
• Fixed Costs (FC): Do NOT change with output (rent, machinery)
• Variable Costs (VC): Change with output (raw materials, labor)
• Total Cost (TC): FC + VC

4.2.2 Total, Average, and Marginal Costs

Cost Type Formula Meaning
Total Fixed Cost (TFC) TFC = Fixed amount Doesn't change with output
Total Variable Cost (TVC) TVC = Changes with output Increases as production increases
Total Cost (TC) TC = TFC + TVC Total of all costs
Average Fixed Cost (AFC) AFC = TFC / Q Fixed cost per unit
Average Variable Cost (AVC) AVC = TVC / Q Variable cost per unit
Average Total Cost (ATC) ATC = TC / Q Total cost per unit
Marginal Cost (MC) MC = ΔTC / ΔQ Cost of producing one more unit

4.2.3 Relationship Between Production and Cost Curves

Key Relationship: When MP is RISING, MC is FALLING.
When MP is at MAXIMUM, MC is at MINIMUM.
When MP is FALLING, MC is RISING.

MC and MP have an INVERSE relationship.
Production Cost Relationship
MP rising MC falling Inverse
MP at maximum MC at minimum Mirror point
MP falling MC rising Inverse

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • TFC = Constant | TVC = Changes with output
  • TC = TFC + TVC
  • AFC = TFC/Q (always decreases)
  • MC = ΔTC/ΔQ
  • MC and MP have INVERSE relationship
  • MC curve intersects ATC and AVC at their MINIMUM points
