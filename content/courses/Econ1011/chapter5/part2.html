5.3 Monopoly Market

5.3.1 Definition and Characteristics

A monopoly is a market structure where there is ONLY ONE seller of a product with NO close substitutes. The monopolist is a PRICE MAKER — it can influence the market price.

Key Characteristics of Monopoly:
1. Single seller — one firm controls the entire market
2. No close substitutes — unique product
3. Price maker — firm sets the price
4. High barriers to entry — new firms cannot enter
5. Imperfect information — firm has more knowledge
6. Downward-sloping demand — must lower price to sell more

5.3.2 Sources of Monopoly

Source Description Example
1. Legal Barriers (Patents) Government grants exclusive rights Pharmaceutical patents
2. Control of Key Resources Firm owns essential input Diamond mines (De Beers)
3. Government Franchise Government grants exclusive license Ethio Telecom (historically)
4. Natural Monopoly Economies of scale make one firm most efficient Electric utilities, water supply
5. Network Effects Value increases with more users Facebook, WhatsApp

💡 Real World Example:

Ethiopian Electric Power (EEP) is a natural monopoly. It would be inefficient to have TWO companies building separate power grids. The high fixed costs mean one company can serve everyone at lower cost.

📌 Key Points:

  • Monopoly: One seller, no substitutes, price maker
  • Barriers to entry create and maintain monopolies
  • Monopolist maximizes profit where MR = MC
  • Monopoly price is HIGHER and quantity LOWER than perfect competition
  • Sources: Patents, resource control, government, natural monopoly
