5.4 Monopolistically Competitive Market

Monopolistic competition is a market structure with MANY firms selling DIFFERENTIATED products. It combines elements of both perfect competition (many firms) and monopoly (product differentiation gives some pricing power).

Key Characteristics of Monopolistic Competition:
1. Many sellers — large number of firms
2. Product differentiation — similar but not identical
3. Free entry and exit — low barriers
4. Some price-setting ability — limited by substitutes
5. Non-price competition — advertising, branding

💡 Real World Examples:

Restaurants, clothing brands, hair salons, coffee shops — many firms, but each offers something slightly different (brand, quality, location, service).

Feature Perfect Competition Monopolistic Competition
Number of firms Many Many
Product Identical Differentiated
Price setting Price taker Some control
Entry barriers None Low
Advertising No need Important

5.5 Oligopoly Market

Oligopoly is a market structure with a FEW large firms dominating the market. These firms are INTERDEPENDENT — each firm's actions affect the others.

Key Characteristics of Oligopoly:
1. Few dominant firms — usually 2-10 firms
2. Interdependence — firms watch each other
3. High barriers to entry — difficult for new firms
4. Products may be identical or differentiated
5. Strategic behavior — game theory applies

💡 Real World Examples:

Oligopoly examples: Mobile phone networks (2-3 providers), airlines, banks, cement manufacturers, automobile industry (Toyota, Honda, Ford).

📌 Chapter 5 Complete Summary:

  • Perfect Competition: Many firms, identical products, price takers
  • Monopoly: One firm, no substitutes, price maker
  • Monopolistic Competition: Many firms, differentiated products
  • Oligopoly: Few firms, interdependent, strategic behavior
  • Market spectrum: Perfect → Monopolistic → Oligopoly → Monopoly
