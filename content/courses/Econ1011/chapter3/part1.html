3.1 Consumer Preferences

Every day, consumers make CHOICES. You decide what to eat, what to wear, what to buy with your limited money. Consumer preferences explain WHY you choose one thing over another.

Key Definition: Consumer preferences describe how a consumer RANKS or ORDERS different bundles of goods based on the satisfaction (utility) they provide.

Consumers face choices because of SCARCITY — they have limited income but unlimited wants. Therefore, they must decide WHICH goods to buy and in WHAT quantities.

The Three Basic Assumptions About Consumer Preferences:

Assumption Meaning Example
1. Completeness Consumer can COMPARE and RANK any two bundles A student can say: "I prefer coffee over tea" OR "I'm indifferent between them"
2. Transitivity If A > B and B > C, then A > C If you prefer coffee over tea, and tea over milk, then you prefer coffee over milk
3. Non-satiation (More is Better) Consumer ALWAYS prefers MORE of a good You'd prefer 2 cups of coffee over 1 cup (if it's free)

💡 Real World Example:

Imagine you have 50 Birr and you're at a café. You can buy:

A) 2 cups of coffee (25 Birr each) + 1 bread (free)
B) 1 cup of coffee + 2 breads
C) 5 breads (10 Birr each)

Your CHOICE reveals your PREFERENCES. If you pick A, you've revealed that you prefer coffee over bread. This is the foundation of consumer theory.

3.2 The Concept of Utility

Utility is the SATISFACTION or PLEASURE a consumer gets from consuming a good or service. It's a MEASURE of happiness or well-being from consumption.

Key Definition: Utility is the satisfaction, pleasure, or benefit a consumer receives from consuming a good or service.

Two Types of Utility:

Type Definition Example
Total Utility (TU) TOTAL satisfaction from consuming ALL units Satisfaction from eating 3 slices of pizza
Marginal Utility (MU) ADDITIONAL satisfaction from ONE more unit Extra satisfaction from the 4th slice

Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility

The Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility: As a consumer consumes MORE units of a good, the ADDITIONAL satisfaction (MU) from each extra unit EVENTUALLY DECREASES.

Key Observations:

  • MU DECREASES as consumption increases (Law of Diminishing MU)
  • When MU = 0, TU is at MAXIMUM (5th unit)
  • When MU is NEGATIVE, TU DECREASES (6th unit)
  • TU increases as long as MU is POSITIVE

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • Consumer preferences: Completeness, Transitivity, Non-satiation
  • Utility = Satisfaction from consumption
  • Total Utility (TU) = Sum of all satisfaction
  • Marginal Utility (MU) = Extra satisfaction from one more unit
  • Law of Diminishing MU: MU decreases as consumption increases
  • When MU = 0, TU is MAXIMUM
  • When MU < 0, TU DECREASES
