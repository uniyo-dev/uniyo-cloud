3.3 Approaches of Measuring Utility

Economists have developed TWO main approaches to measure utility: the Cardinal Utility Theory and the Ordinal Utility Theory.

3.3.1 The Cardinal Utility Theory

The Cardinal Utility Theory (developed by Alfred Marshall) assumes that utility can be MEASURED IN NUMBERS — like measuring weight in kilograms or temperature in degrees.

Key Assumption: Utility can be QUANTIFIED in numerical units called "UTILS." For example: 1 cup of coffee = 20 utils, 1 bread = 10 utils.

Key Concepts in Cardinal Utility:

Concept Formula Meaning
Total Utility (TU) TU = Σ MU Total satisfaction from all units
Marginal Utility (MU) MU = ΔTU / ΔQ Change in satisfaction from one more unit
Consumer Equilibrium MU = Price Maximum satisfaction point

The Law of Equi-Marginal Utility

Consumer Equilibrium Condition:
MUx / Px = MUy / Py

The consumer maximizes satisfaction when the LAST Birr spent on EACH good gives the SAME marginal utility.

💡 Calculation Example:

Suppose you have 10 Birr. Coffee costs 5 Birr, Bread costs 5 Birr.

MU from 1st coffee = 30 utils
MU from 1st bread = 20 utils

MU/P for coffee = 30/5 = 6 utils per Birr
MU/P for bread = 20/5 = 4 utils per Birr

Since coffee gives MORE utility per Birr (6 > 4), buy COFFEE first! Continue until MU/P is equal for both goods.

3.3.2 The Ordinal Utility Theory

The Ordinal Utility Theory (developed by Hicks and Allen) argues that utility CANNOT be measured in numbers. Instead, consumers can only RANK their preferences (1st, 2nd, 3rd, etc.) — like ranking your favorite foods.

Key Assumption: Consumers can RANK bundles (A > B > C) but CANNOT say by HOW MUCH they prefer A over B. Ordinal = ORDER only, NOT magnitude.

Indifference Curves

An Indifference Curve (IC) shows all combinations of two goods that give the consumer the SAME level of satisfaction.

Properties of Indifference Curves:

Property Explanation
1. Downward Sloping More of one good requires less of another
2. Convex to Origin Diminishing Marginal Rate of Substitution
3. Never Intersect Two ICs cannot cross (would violate transitivity)
4. Higher IC = Higher Satisfaction Farther from origin means MORE of both goods

Budget Line (Budget Constraint)

The Budget Line shows all combinations of two goods a consumer can buy with their limited income.

Budget Line Formula:
PxX + PyY = M

Where: Px = Price of X, Py = Price of Y, M = Income

💡 Example:

Income = 100 Birr, Pcoffee = 20, Pbread = 10

Maximum coffee = 100/20 = 5 cups
Maximum bread = 100/10 = 10 loaves

The budget line connects these two points.

📌 Chapter 3 Complete Summary:

  • Cardinal Utility: Utility is MEASURABLE (in utils)
  • Ordinal Utility: Utility can only be RANKED
  • Consumer equilibrium: MU/P is equal for all goods
  • Indifference curves show equal satisfaction
  • Budget line shows affordable combinations
  • Optimal choice: IC tangent to Budget Line
