2.3 Market Equilibrium

Market equilibrium occurs when the quantity demanded equals the quantity supplied at a particular price. At this point, there is NO shortage and NO surplus — the market is in balance.

Key Definition: Market equilibrium is the point where Demand = Supply. The equilibrium price (P*) is where Qd = Qs.

Finding Equilibrium

Given:

  • Demand: Qd = 100 − 2P
  • Supply: Qs = 20 + 2P

At equilibrium: Qd = Qs

100 − 2P = 20 + 2P

100 − 20 = 2P + 2P

80 = 4P

P* = 20 Birr (Equilibrium Price)

Substitute P* = 20 into either equation:

Qd = 100 − 2(20) = 60

Q* = 60 units (Equilibrium Quantity)

What Happens When Price is NOT at Equilibrium?

Situation What Happens Market Force Result
Price ABOVE Equilibrium Qs > Qd (Surplus) Producers reduce price Price falls toward equilibrium
Price BELOW Equilibrium Qd > Qs (Shortage) Consumers bid up price Price rises toward equilibrium

💡 Real World Example:

Coffee Market in Ethiopia:
If the government sets a price ABOVE equilibrium, farmers produce more coffee (surplus), but consumers buy less. The surplus forces prices down.

If the price is BELOW equilibrium, consumers want more coffee but farmers supply less (shortage). The shortage pushes prices up.

Eventually, the market settles at equilibrium where supply meets demand.

Chapter Summary

📌 Chapter 2 Complete Summary:

  • Demand: Inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded
  • Supply: Direct relationship between price and quantity supplied
  • Law of Demand: Price ↑ → Qd ↓
  • Law of Supply: Price ↑ → Qs ↑
  • Equilibrium: Where Demand = Supply
  • Surplus: Price above equilibrium (Qs > Qd)
  • Shortage: Price below equilibrium (Qd > Qs)
  • Elasticity: Measures responsiveness to price changes
