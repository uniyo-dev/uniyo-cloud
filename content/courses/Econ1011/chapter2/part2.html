2.2 Theory of Supply

The Theory of Supply explains the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity that producers are willing and able to sell at various prices during a specific period.

Key Definition: Supply refers to the quantity of a good or service that producers are WILLING and ABLE to offer for sale at various prices during a given time period, holding all other factors constant.

Law of Supply: As price INCREASES, quantity supplied INCREASES (direct relationship). Producers are willing to supply more when prices are higher because they can earn more profit.

B. Supply Curve (Graph)

The supply curve slopes UPWARD from left to right, showing the DIRECT relationship between price and quantity supplied.

C. Supply Function (Equation)

Linear Supply Function:
Qs = c + dP

Where:
Qs = Quantity supplied
c = Intercept (quantity when price is zero)
d = Slope (change in quantity per unit change in price)
P = Price

💡 Example:

If the supply function is: Qs = 20 + 2P

When P = 10 → Qs = 20 + 2(10) = 40

When P = 30 → Qs = 20 + 2(30) = 80

When P = 50 → Qs = 20 + 2(50) = 120

2.2.2 Determinants of Supply

Determinant Effect on Supply Example
1. Production Costs ↑ Costs → ↓ Supply Higher fuel prices → less supply of transported goods
2. Technology Better technology → ↑ Supply Improved farming methods → more crop production
3. Number of Sellers More sellers → ↑ Supply More coffee farmers → more coffee supply
4. Government Policies Taxes → ↓ Supply
Subsidies → ↑ Supply		 Tax on cigarettes → less supply
Subsidy for farmers → more supply
5. Weather Conditions Good weather → ↑ Supply
Bad weather → ↓ Supply		 Good rains → bumper harvest
6. Prices of Related Goods ↑ Price of alternative → ↓ Supply of original If wheat price rises, farmers produce less barley

Increase in Supply: Curve shifts RIGHT

2.2.3 Elasticity of Supply

Price Elasticity of Supply (PES) Formula:
PES = (% Change in Quantity Supplied) / (% Change in Price)
Type Value Meaning
Elastic Supply PES > 1 Producers respond strongly to price changes
Inelastic Supply PES < 1 Producers respond weakly to price changes
Unit Elastic PES = 1 Proportional response

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • Law of Supply: Price ↑ → Quantity Supplied ↑ (direct relationship)
  • Supply curve slopes UPWARD
  • Determinants of supply SHIFT the entire curve
  • Taxes decrease supply, subsidies increase supply
  • Good weather increases agricultural supply
  • Elasticity of supply measures producer responsiveness
