2.1 Theory of Demand

The Theory of Demand is one of the most fundamental concepts in economics. It explains the relationship between the price of a good and the quantity that consumers are willing and able to purchase at various prices during a specific period.

Key Definition: Demand refers to the quantity of a good or service that consumers are WILLING and ABLE to purchase at various prices during a given time period, holding all other factors constant (ceteris paribus).

Important: Demand is NOT just wanting something. You must have:

  • ✅ Desire — You want the good
  • ✅ Ability to pay — You have the money
  • ✅ Willingness to pay — You are ready to spend

💡 Real World Example:

A student may WANT a laptop (desire), but if she has no money, it's not demand. If she has money but is saving for tuition, she has the ability but not willingness. Only when she has desire + ability + willingness does it become DEMAND.

2.1.1 Demand Schedule, Demand Curve, and Demand Function

A. Demand Schedule (Table)

A demand schedule is a table showing the quantity of a good that consumers are willing to buy at different prices during a specific time period.

Observation: As price INCREASES, quantity demanded DECREASES. This is the Law of Demand.

B. Demand Curve (Graph)

When we plot the demand schedule on a graph, we get the demand curve. It slopes DOWNWARD from left to right, showing the inverse relationship between price and quantity demanded.

The demand curve has a negative slope, illustrating the Law of Demand: as price falls, quantity demanded rises, and vice versa.

C. Demand Function (Equation)

A demand function expresses demand mathematically. The general form is:

Linear Demand Function:
Qd = a − bP

Where:
Qd = Quantity demanded
a = Intercept (quantity when price is zero)
b = Slope (change in quantity per unit change in price)
P = Price

💡 Example:

If the demand function is: Qd = 100 − 2P

When P = 10 → Qd = 100 − 2(10) = 80

When P = 30 → Qd = 100 − 2(30) = 40

When P = 50 → Qd = 100 − 2(50) = 0

2.1.2 Determinants of Demand

The determinants of demand are factors OTHER than price that shift the entire demand curve left or right. These include:

Determinant Effect on Demand Example
1. Consumer Income ↑ Income → ↑ Demand (normal goods)
↑ Income → ↓ Demand (inferior goods)		 More income → buy more meat
More income → buy less injera (switch to bread)
2. Price of Related Goods Substitutes: ↑ Price of tea → ↑ Demand for coffee
Complements: ↑ Price of sugar → ↓ Demand for coffee		 Coffee and tea are substitutes
Coffee and sugar are complements
3. Tastes and Preferences Favorable change → ↑ Demand Health campaign says coffee is good → demand increases
4. Population Size ↑ Population → ↑ Demand More students in university → more demand for textbooks
5. Expectations of Future Prices Expect price to rise → ↑ Demand NOW Expect fuel prices to increase → buy fuel today
6. Seasons and Weather Seasonal changes → Changes in demand Rainy season → ↑ demand for umbrellas

Increase in Demand: Curve shifts RIGHT

2.1.3 Elasticity of Demand

Elasticity of demand measures how RESPONSIVE the quantity demanded is to a change in price. It tells us how much quantity changes when price changes.

Price Elasticity of Demand (PED) Formula:
PED = (% Change in Quantity Demanded) / (% Change in Price)

PED = |(ΔQ/Q) / (ΔP/P)|

Types of Price Elasticity:

Type Value Meaning Example
Elastic Demand PED > 1 Quantity changes MORE than price Luxury goods (smartphones, designer clothes)
Inelastic Demand PED < 1 Quantity changes LESS than price Necessities (salt, bread, medicine)
Unit Elastic PED = 1 Quantity changes SAME as price Rare, theoretical
Perfectly Elastic PED = ∞ Any price increase → zero demand Perfect substitutes
Perfectly Inelastic PED = 0 Quantity doesn't change with price Life-saving drugs

💡 Calculation Example:

If price of coffee increases from 20 to 25 Birr (25% increase),
and quantity demanded decreases from 100 to 70 kg (30% decrease),

PED = |(-30%) / (+25%)| = 1.2

Since PED > 1, demand is ELASTIC. Consumers are very responsive to price changes.

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • Demand = Willingness + Ability + Desire to buy
  • Law of Demand: Price ↑ → Quantity Demanded ↓ (inverse relationship)
  • Demand curve slopes DOWNWARD
  • Determinants of demand SHIFT the entire curve
  • Elasticity measures RESPONSIVENESS to price changes
  • Elastic: PED > 1 | Inelastic: PED < 1 | Unit: PED = 1
