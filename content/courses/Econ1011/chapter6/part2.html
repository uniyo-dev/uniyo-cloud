6.3 Nominal versus Real GDP

Key Definitions:
• Nominal GDP: Measured at CURRENT prices
• Real GDP: Measured at CONSTANT (base year) prices
• Real GDP removes the effect of inflation

💡 Example:

If Ethiopia produces 100 units of coffee in 2020 at 10 Birr/kg and in 2024 produces 100 units at 15 Birr/kg:

Nominal GDP 2024 = 100 × 15 = 1,500 Birr
Real GDP 2024 (at 2020 prices) = 100 × 10 = 1,000 Birr

The difference (500 Birr) is due to PRICE INCREASES, not more production.

6.4 GDP Deflator and CPI

GDP Deflator

GDP Deflator Formula:
GDP Deflator = (Nominal GDP / Real GDP) × 100

Measures the overall price level of ALL goods produced domestically.

Consumer Price Index (CPI)

CPI Formula:
CPI = (Cost of basket in current year / Cost of basket in base year) × 100

Measures the price level of a FIXED basket of consumer goods.
Feature GDP Deflator CPI
Goods Covered ALL goods produced Fixed consumer basket
Imports Excludes Includes
Basket Changes each year Fixed
Formula Nominal/Real × 100 Basket cost comparison

💡 Inflation Calculation:

If CPI in 2023 = 110 and CPI in 2024 = 121:

Inflation Rate = (121 - 110) / 110 × 100 = 10%

Prices increased by 10% between 2023 and 2024.

📌 Key Points:

  • Nominal GDP = Current prices | Real GDP = Constant prices
  • GDP Deflator = ALL goods | CPI = Consumer basket only
  • Inflation = % change in price index
  • Real GDP better measures actual production changes
