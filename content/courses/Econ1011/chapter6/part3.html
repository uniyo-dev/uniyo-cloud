6.5 The Business Cycle

The Business Cycle refers to the alternating periods of economic EXPANSION and CONTRACTION in an economy over time.

Phase Characteristics Economic Indicators
Expansion Growing GDP, low unemployment Rising output, increasing employment
Peak Maximum economic activity Full employment, highest output
Recession Declining GDP, rising unemployment Falling output, layoffs
Trough Minimum economic activity Lowest output, highest unemployment
Recovery Economy begins growing again Rising output, job creation

6.6 Macroeconomic Problems

6.6.1 Unemployment

Unemployment Rate Formula:
Unemployment Rate = (Unemployed / Labor Force) × 100

Labor Force = Employed + Unemployed (actively seeking work)
Type Cause Example
Frictional Job search between jobs Graduate looking for first job
Structural Skills mismatch Technology replaces workers
Cyclical Economic recession Layoffs during downturn
Seasonal Seasonal changes Farm workers after harvest

6.6.2 Inflation

Inflation: Sustained INCREASE in the general price level.
Deflation: Sustained DECREASE in the price level.
Disinflation: Decreasing rate of inflation (prices still rising but slower).
Type Cause Example
Demand-Pull Excess demand pulls prices up Government spending surge
Cost-Push Rising production costs Oil price increase

6.6.3 Trade Deficit and Budget Deficit

Trade Deficit: Imports > Exports (X < M)
Budget Deficit: Government spending > Tax revenue (G > T)

📌 Key Points:

  • Business Cycle: Expansion → Peak → Recession → Trough → Recovery
  • Unemployment Rate = Unemployed / Labor Force × 100
  • Inflation = sustained price increase
  • Trade Deficit = Imports > Exports
  • Budget Deficit = Spending > Revenue
