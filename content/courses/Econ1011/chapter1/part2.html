1.4 Scarcity, Choice, Opportunity Cost, and PPF

Scarcity

Scarcity is the fundamental economic problem. It means that resources are LIMITED while human wants are UNLIMITED. This is true for ALL societies — rich or poor.

Scarcity Definition: The condition where available resources are insufficient to satisfy all human wants and needs.

Choice

Because of scarcity, individuals and societies must make CHOICES. Choosing one option means giving up another.

Opportunity Cost

Opportunity Cost: The value of the NEXT BEST ALTERNATIVE FORGONE when a choice is made. It's NOT all alternatives — just the single best one given up.

💡 Example:

A student has 3 hours free. She can: (a) Study Economics, (b) Watch a movie, or (c) Sleep.

If she chooses to study Economics, her opportunity cost is the NEXT BEST alternative — either watching the movie OR sleeping (whichever she values more). It's NOT both.

Production Possibilities Frontier (PPF)

The PPF shows the maximum possible combinations of two goods that can be produced with available resources and technology.

Point Location Meaning
A ON the curve ✅ Efficient — all resources used
B INSIDE the curve ❌ Inefficient — underutilization
C OUTSIDE the curve 🚫 Unattainable — beyond capacity

1.5 Basic Economic Questions

Every society must answer three fundamental questions:

Question Meaning Example
WHAT to Produce? Which goods and services to make Should Ethiopia produce more coffee or more textiles?
HOW to Produce? Which production methods to use Manual labor vs. machines in farming
FOR WHOM to Produce? Who gets the goods produced Distribution based on income, need, or equality

📌 Key Points:

  • Scarcity → Choice → Opportunity Cost
  • Opportunity Cost = NEXT BEST alternative only
  • PPF: On curve = Efficient | Inside = Inefficient | Outside = Unattainable
  • Three questions: WHAT, HOW, FOR WHOM to produce
