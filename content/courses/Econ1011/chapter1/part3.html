1.6 Economic Systems

An economic system is the way a society organizes the production, distribution, and consumption of goods and services. There are three main types:

1.6.1 Capitalist (Market) Economy

In a capitalist economy, resources are owned by PRIVATE individuals and businesses. The market (supply and demand) determines what, how, and for whom to produce.

Key Features of Capitalism: • Private property rights
• Profit motive
• Competition
• Consumer sovereignty
• Minimal government intervention (laissez-faire)

1.6.2 Command (Planned) Economy

In a command economy, resources are owned by the GOVERNMENT or STATE. Central planners decide what, how, and for whom to produce.

Key Features of Command Economy: • Government ownership of resources
• Central planning
• No private property
• Government sets prices
• Examples: North Korea, Former Soviet Union

1.6.3 Mixed Economy

A mixed economy combines elements of BOTH capitalism and command economies. Both the private sector and government play important roles.

Key Features of Mixed Economy: • Both private and public ownership
• Market forces + Government regulation
• Social safety nets
• Examples: Ethiopia, USA, UK, most countries

📌 Key Points:

  • Capitalism = Private ownership + Market forces
  • Command = Government ownership + Central planning
  • Mixed = Both private and government (Ethiopia)
  • Most countries are MIXED economies
