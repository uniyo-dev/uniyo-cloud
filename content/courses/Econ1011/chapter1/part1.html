1.1 Definition of Economics

Economics is one of the oldest and most important social sciences. The term Economics originates from the Greek word 'Oikonomia', which is derived from two words:

  • 'Oikos' = Household
  • 'Nomos' = Management or Law
Key Definition: Economics is a social science that studies how individuals, businesses, governments, and societies make CHOICES to allocate SCARCE RESOURCES to satisfy their UNLIMITED WANTS.

Famous economists have defined economics differently over time:

Economist Definition Focus
Adam Smith (1776) "An inquiry into the nature and causes of the wealth of nations" Wealth creation
Alfred Marshall (1890) "Study of mankind in the ordinary business of life" Human welfare
Lionel Robbins (1932) "Science which studies human behavior as a relationship between ends and scarce means" Scarcity and choice
Paul Samuelson (1948) "Study of how societies use scarce resources to produce valuable commodities" Resource allocation

📌 Quick Memory Tip:

  • Economics = SCARCITY + CHOICE + ALLOCATION
  • All definitions revolve around: Limited resources, Unlimited wants, Making choices

1.2 The Rationales of Economics

Why do we study economics? Economics helps us understand:

  • How markets work — Supply and demand determine prices
  • How to make better decisions — Cost-benefit analysis in daily life
  • How the economy functions — GDP, inflation, unemployment
  • Government policies — Taxes, subsidies, regulations
  • Global trade — International economics and development

💡 Real World Example:

When you decide whether to buy a textbook or a meal with your limited 200 Birr, you are using economic reasoning. You compare the benefits (knowledge vs. hunger satisfaction) and make a choice based on which gives you more utility. This is economics in action!

1.3 Scope and Method of Analysis

1.3.1 Scope of Economics

Economics has two main branches:

Branch Focus Examples
Microeconomics Individual economic units Household spending, firm pricing, market structure
Macroeconomics The economy as a whole GDP, inflation, unemployment, monetary policy

1.3.2 Positive and Normative Analysis

Positive Economics: Deals with FACTS — what IS. Can be tested and proven.
Normative Economics: Deals with OPINIONS — what SHOULD BE. Value judgments.
Type Example Statement Testable?
Positive "Inflation in Ethiopia is 19.2%" ✅ Yes (verify with data)
Positive "Unemployment increased by 2% last year" ✅ Yes
Normative "The government should reduce taxes" ❌ No (opinion)
Normative "Inflation is too high" ❌ No (subjective)

1.3.3 Inductive and Deductive Reasoning

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • Economics = Choice under Scarcity
  • Micro = Individual | Macro = Whole Economy
  • Positive = Facts | Normative = Opinions
  • Inductive = Specific → General | Deductive = General → Specific
