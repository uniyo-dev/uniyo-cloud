2.4 Geological Processes and the Resulting Landforms of Ethiopia and the Horn

Ethiopia and the Horn of Africa have undergone BILLIONS of years of geological processes, each era leaving distinctive rocks and landforms that we see today.

2.4.1 The Precambrian Era (4.5 billion - 600 million years ago)

The Precambrian Era is the OLDEST and LONGEST geological era, spanning about 88% of Earth's history.

Precambrian in Ethiopia:
• Oldest rocks in Ethiopia (basement complex rocks)
• Intense folding and metamorphism
• Formation of crystalline basement rocks (granite, gneiss, schist)
• Found in: Northern Ethiopia (Tigray), Western Ethiopia (Wollega), Southern Ethiopia
Process Resulting Rocks/Landforms
Folding and Metamorphism Granite, Gneiss, Schist (crystalline rocks)
Intense Heat and Pressure Metamorphic basement complex
Erosion over millions of years Exposed ancient rocks at surface

2.4.2 The Paleozoic Era (600 million - 225 million years ago)

The Paleozoic Era was characterized by DENUDATION (wearing away) of the Precambrian mountains and deposition of sedimentary rocks.

Paleozoic in Ethiopia:
• EROSION of Precambrian mountains to flat surfaces (peneplanation)
• Deposition of SANDSTONE and LIMESTONE
• Marine transgressions (shallow seas covered parts of Ethiopia)
• Evidence of early life (trilobites, brachiopods fossils)

During this era, much of Ethiopia was covered by SHALLOW SEAS, leading to the deposition of marine sediments that later became sandstone and limestone.

2.4.3 The Mesozoic Era (225 - 70 million years ago)

The Mesozoic Era (Age of Dinosaurs) saw continued deposition of sedimentary rocks and the beginning of the break-up of Gondwanaland.

Mesozoic in Ethiopia:
• Deposition of SANDSTONE (Adigrat Sandstone)
• Deposition of LIMESTONE (Antalo Limestone)
• Deposition of GYPSUM and SHALE
• Shallow seas covered much of Ethiopia
• Break-up of Gondwanaland began
Rock Formation Composition Location in Ethiopia
Adigrat Sandstone Sandstone (from ancient deserts) Northern Ethiopia (Tigray)
Antalo Limestone Limestone (from ancient seas) Central Ethiopia
Gypsum/Shale Evaporites (from dried seas) Eastern Ethiopia (Ogaden)

2.4.4 The Cenozoic Era (70 million years ago - Present)

The Cenozoic Era is the most IMPORTANT for Ethiopia's current landscape. Three major events shaped the country:

a. Uplifting of the Arabo-Ethiopian Landmass and Outpouring of Lava

Major Uplift Event:
• The Arabo-Ethiopian landmass was UPLIFTED by tectonic forces
• Huge quantities of LAVA (basalt) poured out through fissures
• Formed the ETHIOPIAN PLATEAUX (highlands)
• This lava covered most of Ethiopia, creating the Trap Series basalts

b. Formation of the Rift Valley

The Great Rift Valley:
• Tectonic FORCES pulled the Earth's crust apart
• A long DEPRESSION (rift valley) formed through eastern Africa
• Ethiopia's Rift Valley runs from NE to SW
• The rift is STILL ACTIVE (widening a few mm per year)
Rift Valley Feature Description
Lakes Lake Abaya, Lake Chamo, Lake Ziway, Lake Awasa
Volcanoes Erta Ale, Fantale, Zuqualla
Hot Springs Dallol (hottest place on Earth)
Fault Lines Active earthquake zones

c. Quaternary Volcanism and Deposition

Quaternary Period (last 2.6 million years):
• CONTINUED volcanic activity in the Rift Valley
• Deposition of ALLUVIAL sediments in valley floors
• Formation of lakes (Abaya, Chamo, Ziway, Awasa)
• Erosion of highlands by rivers (Blue Nile, Awash)

📌 Chapter 2 Complete Summary:

  • Precambrian: Oldest rocks, basement complex (granite, gneiss)
  • Paleozoic: Erosion of mountains, deposition of sedimentary rocks
  • Mesozoic: Sandstone, Limestone deposition (shallow seas)
  • Cenozoic: UPLIFT + LAVA = Ethiopian Plateaus
  • Rift Valley: Tectonic forces pulling crust apart
  • Quaternary: Continued volcanism, lake formation, river erosion
