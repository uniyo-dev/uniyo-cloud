4.3 Water Resources: Rivers, Lakes and Sub-Surface Water

Ethiopia is endowed with ABUNDANT water resources including rivers, lakes, and groundwater. These resources are vital for agriculture, hydropower, drinking water, and economic development.

Key Definition: Water resources refer to all sources of water — surface water (rivers, lakes) and groundwater — that are available or potentially available for human use.

4.3.1 The Ethiopian Rivers

Ethiopia has 12 major river basins and numerous smaller rivers. The total annual surface runoff is estimated at 122 billion cubic meters.

4.3.2 The Ethiopian Lakes

Ethiopia has MANY lakes, most of which are located in the RIFT VALLEY and on the highlands. These lakes are important for fishing, tourism, irrigation, and biodiversity.

Major Ethiopian Lakes:

Highland Lakes:
LAKE TANA:
• LARGEST lake in Ethiopia (~3,600 km²)
• Located in NORTH CENTRAL MASSIFS (Amhara)
• Source of ABay (Blue Nile) River
• Contains 37 islands with historic monasteries
• Important for FISHING and TRANSPORT
• Elevation: ~1,800 meters
Rift Valley Lakes:
1. Lake Ziway — Freshwater, fishing, irrigation
2. Lake Langano — Safe for swimming (bilharzia-free)
3. Lake Abijatta — Shallow, soda lake, flamingos
4. Lake Shalla — DEEPEST Rift Valley lake (266m)
5. Lake Awasa — Freshwater, tourism
6. Lake Abaya — LARGEST Rift Valley lake
7. Lake Chamo — Southern Rift, crocodiles, hippos
Crater Lakes:
• Formed in volcanic craters
• Examples: Lake Wonchi, Lake Zengena
• Usually small and deep
• Important for TOURISM

4.3.3 Subsurface (Ground) Water Resources of Ethiopia

Groundwater is water stored UNDERGROUND in aquifers (permeable rock layers). Ethiopia has significant groundwater potential.

Ethiopia's Groundwater:
• Estimated potential: 2.6-6.5 BILLION cubic meters
• Stored in AQUIFERS (porous rock formations)
• Accessed through WELLS and SPRINGS
• Important in DRY regions (Afar, Somali)
• Used for DRINKING water and IRRIGATION
• Depth varies from shallow (hand-dug wells) to deep (boreholes)

4.4 Water Resources Potentials and Development in Ethiopia

Ethiopia has ENORMOUS water resources potential, but development is still LIMITED.

Hydroelectric Potential:
• Estimated potential: 45,000 MW (2nd highest in Africa)
• Currently developed: < 10%
• Major dams: GERD (6,450 MW), Gilgel Gibe III (1,870 MW), Tekeze (300 MW)

Irrigation Potential:
• Potential irrigable land: 3.7 million hectares
• Currently irrigated: < 10%
• Major schemes: Awash Valley, Omo Valley, Wabishebelle
