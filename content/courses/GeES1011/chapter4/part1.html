4.1 Introduction to Drainage Systems

A drainage system refers to the pattern of rivers and streams that drain water from the land surface. Ethiopia, often called the "Water Tower of Africa," has THREE major drainage systems that distribute water to surrounding regions.

Key Definition: A drainage system (or basin) is an area of land where all surface water converges to a single point — a river, lake, or ocean.

4.2 Major Drainage Systems of Ethiopia

Ethiopia's rivers flow in THREE main directions, creating three major drainage systems:

Drainage System Direction of Flow Major Rivers Percentage of Ethiopia
Western Westward (toward Nile) Abay (Blue Nile), Tekeze, Baro-Akobo ~40%
Southeastern Southeastward (toward Indian Ocean) Wabishebelle, Ghenalle ~33%
Rift Valley Inward (into Rift Valley lakes) Awash, Omo, Ghibe ~27%

4.2.1 The Western Drainage System

The WESTERN DRAINAGE SYSTEM is the LARGEST, draining about 40% of Ethiopia's land area. These rivers flow WESTWARD toward the NILE RIVER and eventually to the MEDITERRANEAN SEA.

Major Rivers of the Western System:
1. Abay (Blue Nile) River:
• LONGEST river in Ethiopia (~1,450 km within Ethiopia)
• Originates from LAKE TANA
• Carries ~70% of Nile's water during flood season
• GERD built on this river
• Carves the GRAND CANYON (Blue Nile Gorge)

2. Tekeze River:
• Located in NORTHERN Ethiopia
• Flows through DEEP GORGES in Tigray
• Tekeze Dam built on it
• Joins Atbara River (Sudan) then Nile

3. Baro-Akobo River:
• Located in SOUTHWESTERN Ethiopia
• Flows through GAMBELLA region
• Joins Sobat River (South Sudan) then Nile

4.2.2 The Southeastern Drainage System

The SOUTHEASTERN DRAINAGE SYSTEM drains about 33% of Ethiopia. These rivers flow SOUTHEASTWARD toward the INDIAN OCEAN (through Somalia).

Major Rivers of the Southeastern System:
1. Wabishebelle River:
• LONGEST river entirely within Ethiopia (~1,200 km)
• Originates from BALE HIGHLANDS
• Flows through SOMALI region
• Important for IRRIGATION
• Eventually disappears in Somalia (seasonal)

2. Ghenalle River:
• Originates from BALE HIGHLANDS
• Flows through SOMALI region
• Joins Wabishebelle near Somalia border
• Important for PASTORALISM

4.2.3 The Rift Valley Drainage System

The RIFT VALLEY DRAINAGE SYSTEM is an INTERNAL (endorheic) system — rivers flow INWARD and do NOT reach the ocean. They empty into RIFT VALLEY LAKES.

Major Rivers of the Rift Valley System:
1. Awash River:
• Flows NORTHEAST through the Rift Valley
• Originates near ADDIS ABABA (Shewa Plateau)
• Ends in LAKE ABBE (Afar)
• Important for IRRIGATION (sugarcane, cotton)

2. Omo River:
• Flows SOUTH through Rift Valley
• Originates from SOUTHWESTERN HIGHLANDS
• Ends in LAKE TURKANA (Kenya border)
• GILGEL GIBE dams built on Omo

3. Ghibe River:
• Tributary of Omo River
• Important for hydropower

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • Ethiopia has 3 drainage systems: Western, Southeastern, Rift Valley
  • Western = LARGEST (~40%), flows to Nile
  • Southeastern (~33%), flows to Indian Ocean
  • Rift Valley (~27%), INTERNAL (endorheic)
  • Abay (Blue Nile) = longest river in Ethiopia
  • Wabishebelle = longest river entirely in Ethiopia
  • Awash = ends in Lake Abbe (Afar)
  • Omo = ends in Lake Turkana
