7.3.3 Migration in Ethiopia and the Horn

Migration is the movement of people from one place to another with the intention of settling permanently or temporarily. It is a key component of population dynamics alongside fertility and mortality. In Ethiopia and the Horn of Africa, migration is driven by economic, social, political, and environmental factors. Understanding migration patterns is essential for urban planning, resource allocation, and policy development.

Key Concept: Migration affects population size, composition, and distribution. It can be internal (within a country) or international (across borders). Both types have significant implications for development.

1. Internal Migration

Internal migration refers to the movement of people within the borders of a country. It is the most common form of migration in Ethiopia and significantly shapes population distribution.

Types of Internal Migration in Ethiopia:
  • Rural-Urban Migration:
    • Movement from rural areas to urban centers
    • Major destinations: Addis Ababa, Adama, Dire Dawa, Hawassa, Bahir Dar
    • Drivers: Better employment opportunities, education, healthcare, and social services
    • Impact: Rapid urbanization, pressure on urban infrastructure
  • Rural-Rural Migration:
    • Movement between rural areas
    • Examples: Movement to areas with better agricultural land, resettlement programs
    • Drivers: Land scarcity, environmental degradation, conflict
  • Urban-Urban Migration:
    • Movement between urban centers
    • Examples: Movement to Addis Ababa from other cities for better opportunities
    • Drivers: Employment, education, family ties
  • Seasonal Migration:
    • Temporary movement during specific seasons
    • Examples: Agricultural workers moving to areas with harvest seasons
    • Drivers: Labor demand in agriculture
  • Forced Migration/Displacement:
    • Involuntary movement due to conflict, drought, or disasters
    • Examples: Internal displacement due to ethnic conflict, drought in lowland areas
    • Impact: Creates humanitarian crises, pressure on host communities
Causes of Internal Migration in Ethiopia:
  • Push Factors (Reasons to leave):
    • Poverty and lack of economic opportunities
    • Land scarcity and environmental degradation
    • Conflict and insecurity
    • Drought and climate change impacts
    • Limited access to services (education, health)
  • Pull Factors (Reasons to go to):
    • Better employment opportunities
    • Education and training opportunities
    • Better health services
    • Family reunion
    • Infrastructure and amenities

2. International Migration

International migration involves movement across national borders. Ethiopia is a significant source of international migrants in the Horn of Africa.

Types of International Migration:
  • Emigration (Out-migration):
    • Movement of Ethiopians to other countries
    • Major destinations: Middle East (Saudi Arabia, UAE, Qatar), Europe (UK, Germany, Italy), North America (USA, Canada), and African countries (South Africa, Sudan)
    • Drivers: Economic opportunities, education, family reunion, political factors
    • Scale: Significant; many Ethiopians work in Middle Eastern countries as laborers
  • Immigration (In-migration):
    • Movement of people into Ethiopia
    • Examples: Refugees from Sudan, South Sudan, Eritrea, Somalia
    • Drivers: Conflict, political instability, drought in neighboring countries
    • Scale: Ethiopia hosts over 800,000 refugees, making it one of the largest refugee-hosting countries in Africa
  • Refugee Migration:
    • Movement of people seeking protection from persecution or conflict
    • Ethiopia's Role: Major refugee-hosting country in the Horn of Africa
    • Refugee Populations: Sudanese refugees, South Sudanese refugees, Eritrean refugees, Somali refugees

7.4 Age and Sex Structure of Ethiopian Population

The age and sex structure of a population is a fundamental demographic characteristic that influences economic development, social services, and future population trends. Ethiopia's population structure is characterized by:

  • Young Population:
    • About 40% of the population is under 15 years old
    • High proportion of children creates demand for education and health services
  • Large Working-Age Population:
    • About 55-56% of the population is between 15-64 years
    • Potential for demographic dividend if productively employed
  • Small Elderly Population:
    • Only about 4-5% of the population is 65+ years
    • Low dependency ratio for elderly care compared to developed countries
  • Sex Structure:
    • Slightly more females than males (approximately 50.5% female, 49.5% male)
    • Women have higher life expectancy than men
  • Dependency Ratio:
    • Youth dependency ratio: High (~80-85 children per 100 working-age adults)
    • Elderly dependency ratio: Low (~5-6 elderly per 100 working-age adults)
    • Total dependency ratio: ~85-90 dependents per 100 working-age adults

7.5 Population Distribution in Ethiopia

Population distribution refers to the spatial arrangement of people across a geographic area. Ethiopia's population is unevenly distributed, with high concentrations in the highlands and sparse settlement in the lowlands.

  • Highland Areas: High population density due to:
    • Favorable climate and rainfall
    • Fertile soils for agriculture
    • Historical settlement patterns
    • Better infrastructure and services
  • Lowland Areas: Low population density due to:
    • Arid and semi-arid conditions
    • Limited agricultural potential
    • Pastoral and nomadic livelihoods
  • Urban Areas: Growing population concentration due to:
    • Rural-urban migration
    • Better employment and services
    • Economic opportunities

7.5.1 Measures of Population Distribution

Demographers use various measures to describe and analyze population distribution. These measures help in planning, resource allocation, and policy-making.

1. Population Density
  • Definition: The number of people per unit area of land.
  • Formula: Population Density = Total Population / Total Land Area
  • Ethiopia's Population Density: Approximately 110-115 people per km²
  • Significance: Provides a general measure of population pressure on land
  • Limitation: Crude measure that does not account for variations in land quality or distribution
2. Crude Density
  • Definition: The total population divided by the total land area (same as population density).
  • Formula: Crude Density = Total Population / Total Land Area
  • Ethiopia's Crude Density: ~110-115 people per km²
  • Interpretation: Indicates overall population pressure on the country's land
  • Limitation: Does not consider land quality, climate, or distribution
3. Physiographical Density
  • Definition: The number of people per unit area of agriculturally productive land (arable land).
  • Formula: Physiographical Density = Total Population / Arable Land Area
  • Ethiopia's Physiographical Density: Higher than crude density because much of the land is not arable
  • Significance: More accurate measure of population pressure on agricultural resources
  • Relevance: Important for food security and agricultural planning
4. Agricultural Density
  • Definition: The number of farmers/agricultural population per unit area of arable land.
  • Formula: Agricultural Density = Agricultural Population / Arable Land Area
  • Significance: Measures pressure on land from the agricultural sector
  • Relevance: Important for agricultural development and land use planning
  • Comparison: High agricultural density indicates intensive land use

💡 Real World Example: Population Distribution in Ethiopia

Ethiopia's population distribution illustrates the importance of environmental factors in settlement patterns:

  • Highland Zones: The Ethiopian highlands, which make up about 40% of the country's area, contain approximately 80-85% of the population. Regions like Oromia, Amhara, and Tigray have the highest population densities.
  • Lowland Zones: The lowlands (Afar, Somali, Gambela) have very low population densities, with pastoral and nomadic livelihoods.
  • Urban Centers: Addis Ababa, with its population of over 5 million, is the most densely populated area in Ethiopia.
  • Implications: This uneven distribution creates challenges for regional development, resource allocation, and service delivery.

Key Insight: Understanding population distribution is essential for balanced regional development, infrastructure planning, and resource management.

  • Pull Factors: Employment, education, health services, family reunion.
