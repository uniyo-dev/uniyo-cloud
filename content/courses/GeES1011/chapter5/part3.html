Rainfall Regions of Ethiopia

Ethiopia's complex topography and seasonal wind patterns create DISTINCT rainfall regions, each with its own rainy season and agricultural implications.

Summer Rainfall Region

Summer Rainfall Region (Kiremt-dominant):
• Receives rain mainly during SUMMER (June-August)
• Largest rainfall region in Ethiopia
• Covers CENTRAL, WESTERN, and NORTHERN highlands
• Caused by SOUTHWEST MONSOON
• Supports main cropping season (teff, wheat, barley)
• Key areas: Shewa, Gojjam, Gondar, Wollo, Tigray (parts)

All Year-Round Rainfall Region

All Year-Round Rainfall Region:
• Receives rain THROUGHOUT the year
• Located in SOUTHWESTERN HIGHLANDS
• HIGHEST rainfall in Ethiopia (>2,000mm)
• Supports dense forests and coffee production
• No distinct dry season
• Key areas: Jimma, Illubabor, Wollega, Bench Maji

Autumn and Spring Rainfall Region

Autumn and Spring Rainfall Region (Belg-dominant):
• Receives rain mainly in AUTUMN (September-November) and SPRING (March-May)
• Located in SOUTHERN and SOUTHEASTERN Ethiopia
• SUMMER is relatively DRY here (unlike most of Ethiopia)
• Important for pastoralists (grass regrowth)
• Key areas: Borena, Somali region, southern Oromia

Winter Rainfall Region

Winter Rainfall Region:
• Receives rain mainly during WINTER (December-February)
• Smallest rainfall region
• Located in NORTHERN AFAR and parts of NORTHEASTERN Ethiopia
• Caused by RED SEA influence
• Very limited rainfall (<500mm)
• Key areas: Afar region (near Red Sea coast)

5.4 Agro-ecological Zones of Ethiopia

Agro-ecological zones are areas with similar CLIMATE, SOIL, and ALTITUDE that determine what crops can be grown and what farming systems are appropriate. Ethiopia has FIVE major agro-ecological zones.

The Wurch Zone

WURCH (Alpine) Zone:
• Elevation: Above 3,200m
• Temperature: Below 10°C
• Rainfall: 1,000-2,000mm
• CROPS: Barley (limited), no maize/sorghum
• VEGETATION: Afroalpine (giant lobelia, St. John's wort)
• LAND USE: Pasture, limited cultivation
• Areas: Simien Mountains, Bale Mountains

The Dega Zone

DEGA (Cool Highland) Zone:
• Elevation: 2,300-3,200m
• Temperature: 10-15°C
• Rainfall: 1,000-2,000mm
• CROPS: Barley, wheat, beans, peas
• VEGETATION: Juniper, Hagenia forests
• LAND USE: Cereal farming, livestock
• Areas: North Central Massifs, Arsi, Bale

The Weyna Dega Zone

WEYNA DEGA (Warm Highland) Zone:
• Elevation: 1,500-2,300m
• Temperature: 15-20°C
• Rainfall: 800-1,500mm
• CROPS: Maize, teff, wheat, coffee, chat
• VEGETATION: Mixed forests (Podocarpus)
• LAND USE: MOST INTENSIVE agriculture
• Areas: Addis Ababa, Shewa, Sidama

The Kolla Zone

KOLLA (Warm Lowland) Zone:
• Elevation: 500-1,500m
• Temperature: 20-30°C
• Rainfall: 500-1,000mm
• CROPS: Sorghum, maize, cotton, sesame
• VEGETATION: Acacia woodland, scrub
• LAND USE: Rain-fed and irrigation agriculture
• Areas: Rift Valley, western lowlands

The Berha Zone

BERHA (Hot Desert) Zone:
• Elevation: Below 500m
• Temperature: Above 30°C
• Rainfall: Below 500mm
• CROPS: Limited (with irrigation only)
• VEGETATION: Desert scrub, thorny bushes
• LAND USE: Pastoralism (camels, goats)
• Areas: Afar, Somali lowlands, Dallol

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • Four rainfall regions: Summer, All Year, Autumn/Spring, Winter
  • Summer rainfall = LARGEST region (central/western highlands)
  • All year rainfall = SW Highlands (Jimma, Illubabor)
  • Autumn/Spring = Southern/SE Ethiopia
  • Winter = Northern Afar only
  • Five agro-ecological zones: Wurch, Dega, Weyna Dega, Kolla, Berha
  • Weyna Dega = most intensive agriculture
  • Berha = pastoralism only
