5.3 Spatiotemporal Patterns and Distribution of Temperature and Rainfall in Ethiopia

The spatiotemporal distribution of temperature and rainfall refers to how these climate elements vary across SPACE (different locations) and TIME (seasons, months). Ethiopia's diverse topography creates complex patterns.

Key Definition:
• Spatial: Variation across different LOCATIONS
• Temporal: Variation across different TIMES (seasons, months)
• Spatiotemporal: Combined space AND time variation

5.3.1 Spatiotemporal Distribution of Temperature

Temperature in Ethiopia varies primarily with ALTITUDE and SEASON.

Altitude Zone Elevation Temperature Range Climate Type
Wurch (Alpine) Above 3,200m Below 10°C Cold (frost possible)
Dega (Cool highland) 2,300-3,200m 10-15°C Cool temperate
Weyna Dega (Warm highland) 1,500-2,300m 15-20°C Temperate
Kolla (Warm lowland) 500-1,500m 20-30°C Tropical
Bereha (Hot desert) Below 500m Above 30°C Hot desert

5.3.2 Spatiotemporal Distribution of Rainfall

Ethiopia's rainfall is HIGHLY VARIABLE — both SPATIALLY (location) and TEMPORALLY (seasonally). The country experiences distinct WET and DRY seasons.

Rainfall Region Annual Rainfall Key Areas
HIGH rainfall Above 2,000mm Southwestern Highlands (Jimma, Illubabor)
MODERATE rainfall 1,000-2,000mm Central Highlands (Shewa)
LOW rainfall 500-1,000mm Eastern Highlands
VERY LOW rainfall Below 500mm Lowlands (Afar, Somali)

Seasonal Rainfall Variability in Ethiopia and the Horn

SUMMER (June, July, August) — Kiremt

Summer Season (Kiremt):
• MAIN RAINY SEASON for most of Ethiopia
• Heaviest rainfall in July and August
• Caused by SOUTHWEST MONSOON (from Atlantic/Congo)
• Critical for agriculture (main cropping season)
• Western highlands receive most rain
• Eastern lowlands are DRY during this period

AUTUMN (September, October, November) — Bega/Tseday

Autumn Season:
• TRANSITION from wet to dry
• September can have some rain (end of Kiremt)
• October and November become DRY
• Temperatures begin to cool
• Harvesting season for many crops

WINTER (December, January, February) — Bega

Winter Season (Bega):
• DRY season for most of Ethiopia
• Little to no rainfall
• COOLEST temperatures (especially highlands)
• Frost possible in high altitude areas
• Southeastern Ethiopia may receive some rain

SPRING (March, April, May) — Belg

Spring Season (Belg):
• SHORT rainy season (secondary)
• Especially important in SOUTHERN and SOUTHEASTERN Ethiopia
• Less rain than Kiremt
• Critical for pastoralists (grass regrowth)
• Unreliable — sometimes fails (drought)

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • Five altitudinal zones: Wurch, Dega, Weyna Dega, Kolla, Bereha
  • Higher altitude = Lower temperature
  • Three seasons: Kiremt (wet), Bega (dry), Belg (short rains)
  • Kiremt = Main rainy season (June-August)
  • Bega = Dry season (December-February)
  • Belg = Short rains (March-May)
  • SW Highlands = HIGHEST rainfall (>2,000mm)
  • Afar/Somali lowlands = LOWEST rainfall (<500mm)
