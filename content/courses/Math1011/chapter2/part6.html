2.6 Polynomials, zeros of polynomials, rational functions and their graphs

The functions described in this section frequently occur as mathematical models of real-life situations. For instance, in business, the demand function gives the price per item, \(p\), in terms of the number of items sold, \(x\). The manufacturer's revenue is determined by multiplying the number of items sold (\(x\)) by the price per item (\(p\)). Thus, the revenue function is \(R = xp = x(80 - x^2) = 80x - x^3\). These demand and revenue functions are examples of polynomial functions.

Polynomial Functions

📌 Definition 2.15:

A polynomial function is a function of the form

\[y = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0, \quad a_n \neq 0.\]

Each \(a_i\) is a real number, and \(n\) is a non-negative integer. \(a_n\) is called the leading coefficient. Such a polynomial is said to be of degree \(n\).

Types of Polynomials:

  • Linear: Degree 1 (\(p(x) = a_1x + a_0, a_1 \neq 0\))
  • Quadratic: Degree 2 (\(p(x) = a_2x^2 + a_1x + a_0, a_2 \neq 0\))
  • Cubic: Degree 3 (\(p(x) = a_3x^3 + a_2x^2 + a_1x + a_0, a_3 \neq 0\))

Properties of Polynomial Functions:

  1. The graph of a polynomial is a smooth, unbroken curve (no sharp corners).
  2. If \(p\) is a polynomial of degree \(n\), it has at most \(n\) zeros.
  3. The graph of a polynomial of degree \(n\) can have at most \(n-1\) turning points.
  4. As \(|x|\) gets very large, \(|y|\) gets very large.

Zeros of a Polynomial

The zeros of a polynomial function provide valuable information for sketching its graph. We can find zeros by factorizing, but we need more advanced methods for higher-degree polynomials.

Division Algorithm: Let \(p(x)\) and \(d(x)\) be polynomials with \(d(x) \neq 0\) and \(degree(d) \leq degree(p)\). Then there exist polynomials \(q(x)\) and \(R(x)\) such that \(p(x) = d(x) \cdot q(x) + R(x)\), where either \(R(x) = 0\) or the degree of \(R(x)\) is less than the degree of \(d(x)\).

Remainder Theorem: When a polynomial \(p(x)\) of degree at least 1 is divided by \(x - r\), the remainder is \(p(r)\).

Factor Theorem: \(x - r\) is a factor of \(p(x)\) if and only if \(p(r) = 0\).

Location Theorem: Let \(f\) be a polynomial function and \(a < b\) be real numbers. If \(f(a)f(b) < 0\), then there is at least one zero of \(f\) between \(a\) and \(b\).

Fundamental Theorem of Algebra: If \(p(x)\) is a polynomial of degree \(n > 0\) with complex coefficients, then \(p(x)\) has at least one zero in the complex number system.

Linear Factorization Theorem: Every polynomial of degree \(n \geq 1\) has exactly \(n\) zeros in the complex number system (counting multiplicities).

Rational Root Theorem: Let \(f(x) = a_n x^n + a_{n-1} x^{n-1} + \dots + a_1 x + a_0\) be a polynomial with integer coefficients. If \(\frac{p}{q}\) is a rational root of \(f(x) = 0\) (where \(p\) and \(q\) have no common factor other than \(\pm 1\)), then \(p\) is a factor of \(a_0\) and \(q\) is a factor of \(a_n\).

Rational Functions and Their Graphs

📌 Definition:

A rational function is a function of the form \(f(x) = \frac{n(x)}{d(x)}\), where \(n(x)\) and \(d(x)\) are polynomials and \(d(x) \neq 0\).

Steps to Sketch a Rational Function:

  1. Find the domain and simplify the function.
  2. Find the y-intercept (set \(x = 0\)) and x-intercepts (set \(y = 0\)).
  3. Determine the asymptotes.

Vertical Asymptote (VA): The line \(x = a\) is a VA if \(a \notin Dom(f)\) and \(f(x) \to \pm \infty\) as \(x \to a\).

Horizontal Asymptote (HA): The line \(y = b\) is a HA if \(f(x) \to b\) as \(x \to \pm \infty\).

  • If \(deg(n) < deg(d)\), then \(y = 0\) (the x-axis) is the HA.
  • If \(deg(n) = deg(d)\), then \(y = \frac{a_n}{b_n}\) (ratio of leading coefficients) is the HA.

Oblique (Slant) Asymptote (OA): The line \(y = ax + b\) (with \(a \neq 0\)) is an OA if \(f(x) \to ax + b\) as \(x \to \pm \infty\). This occurs only when \(deg(n) = deg(d) + 1\).

Example 2.42: Sketch \(f(x) = \frac{x+2}{x-1}\).

  • Domain: \(\mathbb{R} \setminus \{1\}\)
  • y-intercept: \((0, -2)\), x-intercept: \((-2, 0)\)
  • VA: \(x = 1\)
  • HA: \(y = 1\)

📌 Cheat Notes: Key Points to Remember (Part 6)

  • Polynomial: \(y = a_n x^n + \dots + a_0\). Domain = \(\mathbb{R}\). Degree = highest power.
  • Zeros: Found using factorization, Remainder Theorem, Factor Theorem, Rational Root Theorem.
  • Key Theorems: Remainder, Factor, Location, Fundamental Theorem of Algebra, Linear Factorization, Rational Root.
  • Rational Function: \(f(x) = \frac{n(x)}{d(x)}\).
  • Asymptotes: VA (denominator = 0), HA (end behavior), OA (slant, when degree numerator = degree denominator + 1).
