1.1 What is Logic?

Logic is the study of correct reasoning. It is a branch of philosophy that helps us distinguish between valid and invalid arguments. Logic provides us with tools to evaluate whether our thinking is sound and our conclusions follow from our premises.

Key Definition: Logic is the science of reasoning, concerned with determining whether an argument is valid or invalid based on its FORM (structure), not its CONTENT (meaning).

Logic is essential because it helps us:

  • Think clearly — Avoid confusion and ambiguity
  • Argue effectively — Present convincing reasons
  • Detect fallacies — Identify flawed reasoning
  • Solve problems — Break complex issues into manageable parts
  • Make better decisions — Evaluate evidence objectively

💡 Real World Example:

Imagine your friend says: "All students who study hard pass their exams. Abebe studies hard. Therefore, Abebe will pass his exams." This is a logical argument! Logic helps you verify whether this conclusion actually follows from the premises.

1.2 The Branches of Logic

Logic has two main branches:

Branch Focus Example
Formal Logic Structure of arguments All A are B. X is A. Therefore, X is B.
Informal Logic Content and context Detecting emotional appeals, false analogies

1.3 Basic Concepts in Logic

1.3.1 Propositions

A proposition (or statement) is a declarative sentence that is either TRUE or FALSE, but not both.

Examples of Propositions:
✅ "Addis Ababa is the capital of Ethiopia." (TRUE)
✅ "2 + 2 = 5" (FALSE)
❌ "Close the door!" (Command - not a proposition)
❌ "What time is it?" (Question - not a proposition)

1.3.2 Arguments

An argument is a set of propositions where one proposition (the conclusion) is claimed to follow from the others (the premises).

📝 Structure of an Argument:

Premise 1: All universities in Ethiopia offer freshman courses.
Premise 2: Addis Ababa University is a university in Ethiopia.
Conclusion: Therefore, Addis Ababa University offers freshman courses.

1.3.3 Validity and Soundness

Term Definition Question to Ask
Valid Argument IF premises are true, conclusion MUST be true "Does the conclusion follow?"
Sound Argument Valid argument + ALL premises ARE true "Are the premises actually true?"

📌 Key Points to Remember:

  • Logic = Study of correct reasoning
  • Proposition = Statement that is TRUE or FALSE
  • Argument = Premises → Conclusion
  • Valid = Form is correct | Sound = Valid + True premises

1.4 Why Study Logic in University?

As a freshman, logic is your foundation for:

  • 📚 Academic success — Writing clear essays, evaluating research
  • 🗣️ Debate and discussion — Constructing and analyzing arguments
  • 🔬 Scientific thinking — Forming hypotheses and testing them
  • 💼 Professional skills — Problem-solving, critical thinking
  • 🌍 Citizenship — Making informed decisions about social issues

💡 Real World Example:

During student elections, candidates make arguments. Logic helps you evaluate: "Does this candidate's conclusion follow from their evidence? Are their premises true? Is this a valid or sound argument?" This protects you from manipulation and poor choices.
