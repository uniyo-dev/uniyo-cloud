The module presents two different frameworks for understanding the levels of AI: The 7 Layers of AI Maturity (based on Figure 3.4) and The 6 Stages of AI Development (based on the textual progression).

The Seven Levels of AI Maturity

Figure 3.4 in the module presents a layered model showing the progression of AI capabilities. Each layer builds upon the previous one, adding more complexity and intelligence.

LevelLayer NameCore QuestionDescription
1 Perception What's happening now? The most basic level. The AI system perceives its environment through sensors (cameras, microphones) and identifies the current state of the world.
2 Notification What do I need to know? The AI filters the perceived data and notifies the user of important events or changes in the environment.
3 Suggestion What do you recommend? The AI analyzes the data and provides recommendations or suggestions to the user based on patterns and historical data.
4 Automation What should I always do? The AI automates routine tasks and decisions that do not require human intervention, increasing efficiency.
5 Prediction What can I expect to happen? The AI uses predictive analytics to forecast future events or outcomes based on current and historical data.
6 Prevention What can I avoid? The AI takes proactive measures to prevent negative outcomes (e.g., predicting and preventing equipment failure).
7 Situational Awareness What do I need to do right now? The AI fully understands the current context, predicts future states, and takes autonomous action in real-time, adapting to changes.

The 6 Stages of AI Development

Beyond the maturity layers, the module also outlines a deeper progression based on how AI learns, reasons, and interacts with the world. These stages are:

StageNameDescriptionExamples
1 Rule-Based Systems The most common use of AI today fits in this bracket. These systems follow a set of pre-programmed rules to perform specific tasks. They cannot learn beyond their programming. Business software (Robotic Process Automation), domestic appliances (thermostats, washing machines), aircraft autopilots.
2 Context Awareness and Retention Algorithms that develop information about the specific domain they are being applied in. They are trained on the knowledge and experience of the best humans, and their knowledge base can be updated as new situations and queries arise. Chatbots that remember previous conversations, virtual assistants like Siri or Alexa.
3 Domain-Specific Expertise Going beyond the capability of humans, these systems build up expertise in a specific context, taking in massive volumes of information which they can use for decision-making. Successful use cases have been seen in cancer diagnosis and the well-known game of Go (AlphaGo). Currently, this type is limited to one domain only; if you started to teach it something else, it would forget all it knows about that domain. AlphaGo (playing Go), IBM Watson for medical diagnosis.
4 Reasoning Machines These algorithms have some ability to attribute mental states to themselves and others—they have a sense of beliefs, intentions, knowledge, and how their own logic works. This means they could reason or negotiate with humans and other machines. At the moment, these systems are still in the research phase. Project Debater (IBM's AI that debates humans).
5 Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) These systems have human-like intelligence—the most commonly portrayed AI in media. However, no such use is in evidence today. It is the goal of many working in AI and some believe it could be realized already from 2024. A machine that can learn any intellectual task that a human being can.
6 Artificial Superintelligence (ASI) / Singularity AI algorithms that can outsmart even the most intelligent humans in every domain. Logically it is difficult for humans to articulate what the capabilities might be, yet we would hope examples would include solving problems we have failed to solve so far, such as world hunger and dangerous environmental change. Views vary as to when and whether such a capability could even be possible. A hypothetical machine that surpasses all human intelligence and creativity.
📌 The Concept of Singularity (Stage 7):

The module mentions a Stage 7, which is the idea that development provided by ASI (Stage 6) leads to a massive expansion in human capability. Human augmentation could connect our brains to each other and to a future successor of the current internet, creating a "hive mind" that shares ideas, solves problems collectively, and even gives others access to our dreams as observers or participants.

Ray Kurzweil, Google's Director of Engineering, suggests we could see it in a range of science and technology disciplines. The other side of the fence argues that singularity is impossible and human consciousness could never be digitized.

📌 Cheat Notes: Key Points to Remember (Part 3)

  • 7 Maturity Levels: Perception → Notification → Suggestion → Automation → Prediction → Prevention → Situational Awareness.
  • 6 Development Stages:
    • Rule-Based: Follows fixed rules (Autopilots, RPA).
    • Context Awareness: Remembers previous interactions (Chatbots).
    • Domain Expertise: Master of one domain (AlphaGo, Watson).
    • Reasoning Machines: Understands beliefs and intentions (Project Debater).
    • AGI: Human-like intelligence (Theoretical).
    • ASI / Singularity: Outsmarts all humans (Theoretical).
