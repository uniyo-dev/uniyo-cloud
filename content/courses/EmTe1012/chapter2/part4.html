What is Big Data?

Big data is a blanket term for the non-traditional strategies and technologies needed to gather, organize, process, and gather insights from large datasets. While the problem of working with data that exceeds the computing power or storage of a single computer is not new, the pervasiveness, scale, and value of this type of computing have greatly expanded in recent years.

In this section, we will talk about big data on a fundamental level and define common concepts you might come across. We will also take a high-level look at some of the processes and technologies currently being used in this space.

2.5.1. What Is Big Data?

Definition: Big data is the term for a collection of data sets so large and complex that it becomes difficult to process using on-hand database management tools or traditional data processing applications.

Understanding "Large Dataset": In this context, a "large dataset" means a dataset too large to reasonably process or store with traditional tooling or on a single computer. This means that the common scale of big datasets is constantly shifting and may vary significantly from organization to organization.

💡 Real World Example (Ethiopia - Shifting Scales):

  • For a small retail shop in Addis Ababa: A dataset of 10,000 sales transactions might be considered "large" and difficult to analyze in Excel.
  • For Ethio Telecom: A "large dataset" means billions of call detail records (CDRs) generated every day by millions of subscribers across Ethiopia.
  • For a global tech company like Google: A "large dataset" means petabytes (1,000,000 GB) of search queries and user behavior data.

This illustrates that the definition of "big data" is relative and context-dependent.

The 4 Vs of Big Data:

Big data is characterized by 3V and more. The module explicitly lists the following 4 Vs:

VCharacteristicDescriptionExample
Volume Large amounts of data Data is generated at an unprecedented scale. We are talking about Zeta bytes (10²¹ bytes) or Massive datasets. Traditional storage cannot handle this alone. Ethio Telecom generates terabytes of data daily from calls, SMS, and mobile internet usage.
Velocity Data is live streaming or in motion Data is being generated and updated in real-time. It is not static; it is constantly flowing like a river. Systems must process this data in milliseconds. TeleBirr processes thousands of transactions per second in real-time across Ethiopia.
Variety Data comes in many different forms from diverse sources Data is not just tables. It includes structured (SQL), unstructured (text, video, audio), and semi-structured (JSON, XML) data from diverse sources. An Ethiopian agricultural dataset includes structured crop yields, unstructured drone videos, and semi-structured weather JSON files.
Veracity Can we trust the data? How accurate is it? Data comes with uncertainty due to data inconsistency, incompleteness, latency, deception, model approximations, or errors. Veracity is about the quality and trustworthiness of data. A farmer reports a crop yield of "50 quintals" on a mobile app, but the actual yield is 48. The data has veracity issues (it is slightly inaccurate).
📌 Why Traditional Tools Fail:

Traditional tools like Excel, standard SQL databases (RDBMS), and single-server setups cannot handle the 4 Vs effectively. They crash under Volume, lag behind Velocity, reject Variety, and cannot verify Veracity at scale. This is why Clustered Computing and the Hadoop Ecosystem (which we will cover in Part 5) are essential for Big Data processing.

📌 Cheat Notes: Key Points to Remember (Part 4)

  • Big Data Definition: Datasets so large and complex that traditional tools cannot process them.
  • "Large Dataset" is Relative: What is "big" varies by organization (e.g., local shop vs. Ethio Telecom vs. Google).
  • The 4 Vs:
    • Volume: Massive scale (Zeta bytes).
    • Velocity: Real-time streaming data.
    • Variety: Diverse formats (structured, unstructured, semi-structured).
    • Veracity: Trustworthiness and accuracy of data.
  • Traditional Tools Fail: Single computers and RDBMS cannot handle the 4 Vs at scale.
