Why is Data Called the "New Oil"?

In the modern era, data is regarded as the new oil and strategic asset. Just as oil powered the machines and factories of the Industrial Revolution, data powers the algorithms, artificial intelligence, and digital services of today. We are living in the age of big data, where vast amounts of information are generated every second—from social media posts and online purchases to sensor readings and GPS signals. Data drives, and even determines, the future of science, technology, the economy, and possibly everything in our world today and tomorrow.

Data-Driven Innovation and Economic Opportunities:

Data has not only triggered tremendous hype and buzz in the media and tech industry, but more importantly, it presents enormous challenges that, in turn, bring incredible innovation and economic opportunities. Companies that effectively harness data gain a competitive edge. For example, Amazon uses customer purchase data to recommend products, and Netflix uses viewing data to recommend movies and even produce new series based on what people like.

💡 Real World Example (Ethiopia):

In Ethiopia, TeleBirr and other mobile payment platforms generate massive amounts of transaction data. This data reveals spending habits, peak transaction times, and geographic distribution of users. This data-driven insight allows financial institutions to design better financial products, such as micro-loans and savings plans, tailored specifically to Ethiopian consumers. Similarly, the Ethiopian government uses agricultural data (weather patterns, soil conditions, crop yields) to help farmers in regions like Oromia and Amhara make better planting decisions, increasing food security.

Reshaping and Paradigm-Shifting:

This reshaping and paradigm-shifting are driven not just by the data itself, but by all other aspects that could be created, transformed, and/or adjusted by understanding, exploring, and utilizing data. It's not enough to just collect data; the real value lies in analytics—the ability to ask the right questions, find patterns, and make predictions. This shift is affecting every sector:

  • Healthcare: Data from wearable devices and electronic health records helps doctors predict diseases before symptoms appear.
  • Transportation: Data from GPS and traffic cameras is used to optimize traffic lights and reduce congestion.
  • Education: Data on student performance helps teachers identify struggling students and tailor their instruction.

The Fourth Industrial Revolution and Data:

The preceding trend—the exponential growth of data—and its potential have triggered new debate about data-intensive scientific discovery as an emerging technology, the so-called "Fourth Industrial Revolution". The First Industrial Revolution used water and steam power. The Second used electricity. The Third used digital electronics and computers. The Fourth is defined by data, artificial intelligence, and connectivity. In this revolution, data is not just a byproduct; it is the raw material that fuels AI algorithms, trains machine learning models, and enables everything from autonomous vehicles to smart cities.

📌 The Data-Science Connection:

There is no doubt, nevertheless, that the potential of data science and analytics to enable data-driven theory, economy, and professional development is increasingly being recognized. This involves not only core disciplines such as computing, informatics, and statistics, but also the broad-based fields of business, social science, and health/medical science.

  • Computing & Informatics: Building the infrastructure to store and process data (databases, cloud servers).
  • Statistics: Developing the mathematical models to analyze data and find patterns.
  • Business: Using data to improve marketing, supply chains, and customer experience.
  • Social Science: Using data to understand human behavior, migration patterns, and social trends.
  • Health/Medical Science: Using data to track disease outbreaks (like COVID-19), develop new drugs, and personalize treatment.

💡 Real World Example (Ethiopia - Data in Agriculture):

Ethiopia is a predominantly agricultural country. Data science is now being applied to help farmers. For example, the Ethiopian government, in partnership with organizations like the International Food Policy Research Institute (IFPRI), uses satellite data, weather data, and soil data to predict crop yields and warn farmers about droughts. This data-driven approach allows the country to better manage food security and allocate resources to areas most in need. This is a perfect example of data science transforming a core sector of the Ethiopian economy.
