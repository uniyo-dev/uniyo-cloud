The first Augmented Reality Systems (ARS) were usually designed with a basis on three main blocks, as is illustrated in Figure 5.7:

  1. Infrastructure Tracker Unit
  2. Processing Unit
  3. Visual Unit

The Three Main Blocks of ARS

BlockFunctionData Flow / Key Actions
1. Infrastructure Tracker Unit Responsible for collecting data from the real world, sending them to the Processing Unit. Uses sensors, cameras, and GPS to gather real-world positioning and orientation data.
2. Processing Unit Mixes the virtual content with the real content and sends the result to the Video Out module of the Visual Unit. Receives real-world data from the Tracker. Takes visual content from the system. Merges them into a single "Mixed Realities" output.
3. Visual Unit Consists of a Video In (to acquire required data for the Infrastructure Tracker Unit) and a Video Out (to display the mixed realities to the user). Video In captures the real world. Video Out displays the augmented view.

Visualization Technologies: Video See-Through vs. Optical See-Through

The Visual Unit can be classified into two types of systems, depending on the visualization technology used:

TechnologyDescriptionKey Feature
Video see-through Uses a Head-Mounted Display (HMD) that employs a video-mixing and displays the merged images on a closed-view HMD. The user sees the real world through a video feed on a screen inside the headset.
Optical see-through Uses an HMD that employs optical combiners to merge the images within an open-view HMD. The user sees the real world directly through the glasses, and digital content is projected onto the lenses.
📌 The Limitations of HMDs (Head-Mounted Displays):

HMDs are currently the dominant display technology in the AR field. However, they lack in several aspects, such as ergonomics, high prices, and relatively low mobility due to their sizes and connectivity features.

An additional problem involving HMDs is the interaction with the real environment, which places virtual interactive zones to the user, making the collision with these zones hard due to the difficulty to interact with multiple points in different depths.

Alternative Approaches:

  • Monitors: Used as an option for indirect view since the user does not look directly into the mixed world.
  • Tablets: Used in direct view since the user points the camera to the scene and looks directly into the mixed world.

Both approaches (monitors and tablets) still have difficulties in getting a collision.

📌 Cheat Notes: Key Points to Remember (Part 2)

  • 3 Main Blocks: Infrastructure Tracker Unit (collects data), Processing Unit (mixes virtual + real), Visual Unit (Video In/Out).
  • Video See-through: Uses a closed-view HMD; the user sees the real world through a video feed.
  • Optical See-through: Uses an open-view HMD; the user sees the real world directly with digital overlays.
  • HMD Limitations: Poor ergonomics, high cost, low mobility, and difficulty interacting with virtual objects at different depths.
  • Alternatives: Monitors (indirect view) and Tablets (direct view) are used to avoid HMD issues.
